# **2.9.4 Effects of Hydrogen Bonding**

## **1. Higher boiling points than similar substances without hydrogen bonding**

Hydrogen bonding causes some simple molecular substances to have unusually high boiling points compared with similar molecules that cannot form hydrogen bonds.

<div id="effects-hbond-boiling-graph"></div>

### **Why $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ has a higher boiling point than $\text{H}_2\text{S}$, $\text{H}_2\text{Se}$ and $\text{H}_2\text{Te}$**

- $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ molecules can form hydrogen bonds.
- $\text{H}_2\text{S}$, $\text{H}_2\text{Se}$ and $\text{H}_2\text{Te}$ cannot form hydrogen bonds. Their intermolecular forces are mainly instantaneous dipole-induced dipole forces and permanent dipole-permanent dipole interactions.
- Hydrogen bonds are stronger than these weaker intermolecular forces.
- Therefore, more energy is needed to overcome the intermolecular forces between $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ molecules.
- Hence, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ has a much higher boiling point.

### **Why the boiling point order is $\text{NH}_3 < \text{HF} < \text{H}_2\text{O}$**

- $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ has two lone pairs and two electron-deficient hydrogen atoms per molecule.
- Therefore, each $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ molecule can form more extensive hydrogen bonding.
- More energy is needed to overcome the more extensive hydrogen bonding in water.
- Hence, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ has the highest boiling point among $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{HF}$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
- Fluorine is more electronegative than nitrogen.
- Therefore, the H atom in $\text{H--F}$ is more partially positive than the H atom in $\text{N--H}$.
- Hydrogen bonding between $\text{HF}$ molecules is stronger than hydrogen bonding between $\text{NH}_3$ molecules.
- Therefore, $\text{HF}$ has a higher boiling point than $\text{NH}_3$.

## **2. Intermolecular and intramolecular hydrogen bonding**

!!! definition "Definition"
    Intramolecular hydrogen bonding occurs when an electron-deficient hydrogen atom in a molecule is close enough to a lone pair on an F, O or N atom within the same molecule.

<div id="effects-hbond-nitrophenol"></div>

### **Why 2-nitrophenol has a lower boiling point than 4-nitrophenol**

- 2-nitrophenol can form intramolecular hydrogen bonding.
- Therefore, its intermolecular hydrogen bonding is less extensive.
- Less energy is needed to overcome the less extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding between 2-nitrophenol molecules.
- Hence, 2-nitrophenol has a lower boiling point.
- 4-nitrophenol cannot form the same intramolecular hydrogen bond, so it forms more extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding and has a higher boiling point.

During boiling, the main forces overcome are the intermolecular forces between molecules.

## **3. Dimerisation of carboxylic acids in non-polar solvents**

<div id="effects-hbond-carboxylic-dimer"></div>

- In a non-polar solvent such as benzene, carboxylic acid molecules can form dimers through hydrogen bonding.
- Each pair of carboxylic acid molecules is held together by two hydrogen bonds.
- This causes the apparent relative molecular mass to be about twice the expected value.
- For ethanoic acid, $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$, the expected $M_r$ is 60, but the observed apparent $M_r$ in a non-polar solvent may be about 120.

## **4. Ice is less dense than liquid water**

<div id="effects-hbond-ice-water"></div>

- In ice, each $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ molecule is hydrogen bonded to four other $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ molecules in a roughly tetrahedral arrangement.
- This forms an open lattice with empty spaces between the molecules.
- In liquid water, the hydrogen-bonding arrangement is more random and less open.
- The $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ molecules are, on average, closer together in liquid water.
- For the same mass, ice occupies a larger volume than liquid water.
- Hence, ice has a lower density than liquid water.

## **5. Water has high surface tension**

<div id="effects-hbond-surface-tension"></div>

- Water has strong hydrogen bonding between its molecules.
- At the surface, water molecules are attracted strongly to neighbouring water molecules.
- A relatively large force is needed to break or disturb this surface layer.
- Hence, water has a high surface tension.
- This helps explain why small insects such as pond skaters can move on water and why small objects may rest on the water surface if placed carefully.
