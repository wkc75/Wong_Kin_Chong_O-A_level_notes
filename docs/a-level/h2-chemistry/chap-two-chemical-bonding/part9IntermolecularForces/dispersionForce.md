# **Dispersion Force** (Instantaneous Dipole - Induced Dipole Interaction)

Dispersion forces exist between **all** molecules or between **noble gas atoms**. It is the only intermolecular force of attraction between **non polar** molecules. 

## **How Dispersion Force Arises** 

<div id="dispersion-force-viewer"></div>

- The electrons in a molecule are constantly moving
- Fluctuations in electron distribution can lead to **instantaneous dipole**. (For example, all electrons might move to one side of the molecule, resulting in instantaneous dipoles)
- The instantaneous dipole then **induces** a dipole in a nearby molecule. 
- The electrostatic force of attraction between the instantaneous dipole and induced dipole is the **dispersion force**
- Dispersion force is usually **weak** and **short-lived**

## **Strength of dispersion forces**

### **The number of electrons in the molecule**
The **large** the number of electrons, the **more polarisable** the electron cloud, the **stronger** the dispersion force.

### **The surface area of contact between adjacent molecules**
The **large** the surface area of contact, the more easily induced dipoles are formed, the **stronger** the dispersion forces. 

The surface area of contact between molecules depends on the shape of the molecules. Straight chained hydrocarbons have large surface area as compared to branched hydrocarbons.
