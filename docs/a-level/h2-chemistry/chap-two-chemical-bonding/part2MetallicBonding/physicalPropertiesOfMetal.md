# **Physical Properties of Metals**

## **High melting and boiling points**

- Boiling and Melting metals needs to **overcome the metallic bonding**
- Metal has **strong metallic bonding** between the **lattice of positive ions** and the **negatively charged sea of delocalised electrons**.
- **Large amount of energy** is required to overcome the strong metallic bonding.
- Therefore, metals have high boiling and melting points.

<!--prettier-ignore-->
!!! Tip
    To compare the boiling point and melting point of different metals

    Compare the strength of their metallic bonding by comparing the
    1. **number of valence electrons contributed per metal atom** and the
    2. **charge and redius of the metal cation**.

    The metal with stronger metallic bonding has a higher boiling and melting point.

## **Good electrical and thermal conductivity**

- Metal has a **giant metallic lattice structure** which consists of a lattice of positively charged ions and a negatively charged sea of **delocalised electrons**.
- The delocalised electrons can act as **mobile charge carriers** to conduct electricity
- The delocalised electrons also transfer heat quickly throughout the metal via conduction.

## **Malleable and ductile**

**Malleable** describes materials that can be hammered, pressed, or **rolled into thin sheets without breaking**

**Ductile** describes the ability to be stretched, bent or **drawn into thin wires without breaking**

- When a strong enough force is applied, the layers of ions will slide over one another into new positions.
- The shape of metal changes but it does not break because the **sea of delocalised electrons prevent repulsion between the cations** and the **metallic bonding remains intact**.

## **Hardness**

- Metals have **strong metallic bonding** between the **lattice of positive ions** and the **negatively charged sea of delocalised electrons**.
- **A strong force** is required to overcome the strong metallic bonding.
- Thus metals are hard.
