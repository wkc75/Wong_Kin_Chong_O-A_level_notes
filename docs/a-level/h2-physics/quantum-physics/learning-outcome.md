# **H2 Physics: Quantum Physics**

Quantum physics explains observations that cannot be described fully by either a wave model or a particle model alone.

By the end of this chapter, you should be able to connect the evidence, equations and exam language for photons, matter waves, wavefunctions, quantisation and line spectra.

## **Learning Outcomes**

|       | Learning Outcomes |
| ----- | ----------------- |
| 19(a) | show an understanding that the existence of a threshold frequency in the photoelectric effect supports the particulate nature of electromagnetic radiation, while interference and diffraction support its wave nature |
| 19(b) | state that a photon is a quantum of electromagnetic radiation, and recall and use $E = hf$ |
| 19(c) | show an understanding that a photon is massless but has momentum given by $p = \frac{E}{c}$ and $p = \frac{h}{\lambda}$ |
| 19(d) | show an understanding that electron diffraction and double-slit interference of single particles support the wave nature of particles |
| 19(e) | recall and use $\lambda = \frac{h}{p}$ for the de Broglie wavelength |
| 19(f) | show an understanding that the state of a particle can be represented by a wavefunction $\psi$, and that $\lvert \psi \rvert^2$ is the probability density, including normalisation factors for square and sinusoidal wavefunctions |
| 19(g) | show an understanding that superposition applies to wavefunctions, leading to standing waves for a particle in a box and single-particle interference |
| 19(h) | show an understanding that $\Delta x \Delta p \gtrsim h$ relates to the need for a spread of momenta for localised particles, and apply this to solve problems |
| 19(i) | show an understanding of standing wave solutions $\psi_n$ for a particle in a one-dimensional infinite square well |
| 19(j) | solve problems using $E_n = \frac{h^2 n^2}{8mL^2}$ for a particle in a one-dimensional infinite square well |
| 19(k) | show an understanding of discrete electronic energy levels in isolated atoms and deduce how this leads to spectral lines |
| 19(l) | distinguish between emission and absorption line spectra |
| 19(m) | solve problems involving photon absorption or emission during atomic energy level transitions |

## **What to Master**

- Know which experiments support the wave model and which support the photon or particle model.
- Use photon and de Broglie equations accurately with SI units.
- Explain $\psi$ and $\lvert \psi \rvert^2$ without treating the wavefunction as a physical cloud of matter.
- Recognise that boundary conditions lead to allowed standing waves and discrete energies.
- Link energy level transitions to emission and absorption spectra using $hf = \Delta E$.

!!! warning "Common misconception"
    This chapter is not only about photons. The newer syllabus also expects wavefunctions, normalisation, superposition, uncertainty, and particle-in-a-box energy levels.

!!! question "Checkpoint"
    Before revising the details, sort the chapter into three ideas: evidence for quantum behaviour, wavefunctions for particles, and quantised energy in matter.
