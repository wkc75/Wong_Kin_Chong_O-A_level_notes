# **Quantum Physics: Chapter Overview**

## **Big Idea**

Quantum physics links the wave and particle models.

Light can behave as waves in interference and diffraction, but as photons in the photoelectric effect. Particles such as electrons can also show wave behaviour. When matter waves are confined, only certain standing waves are allowed, giving discrete energies.

## **Key Equations**

| Equation | Use |
| -------- | --- |
| $E = hf$ | photon energy |
| $E = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$ | photon energy from wavelength |
| $p = \frac{E}{c}$ | photon momentum from energy |
| $p = \frac{h}{\lambda}$ | photon momentum from wavelength |
| $\lambda = \frac{h}{p}$ | de Broglie wavelength |
| $\int \lvert \psi \rvert^2 dx = 1$ | normalisation condition |
| $\psi_n(x) = A \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$ | standing wave in an infinite square well |
| $L = \frac{n\lambda}{2}$ | allowed wavelengths in a box |
| $E_n = \frac{h^2 n^2}{8mL^2}$ | allowed energy levels in a box |
| $\Delta x \Delta p \gtrsim h$ | position-momentum uncertainty estimate |
| $hf = \Delta E$ | photon energy in atomic transitions |

## **Common Exam Themes**

- Explain why threshold frequency is evidence for photons.
- Compare wave evidence and particle evidence without saying one model is always correct.
- Calculate photon energy, photon momentum and de Broglie wavelength.
- Interpret $\lvert \psi \rvert^2$ as probability per unit length.
- Normalise simple square or sinusoidal wavefunctions.
- Use boundary conditions to decide which standing waves are allowed.
- Calculate energy gaps and wavelengths for atomic transitions.

!!! warning "Common misconception"
    Quantum physics does not mean light is sometimes "really a wave" and sometimes "really a particle". The model used depends on the experiment.

!!! question "Checkpoint"
    Which two observations support the wave nature of electromagnetic radiation, and which observation supports the photon model?
