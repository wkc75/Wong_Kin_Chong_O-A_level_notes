# **19.2 Photons, Energy and Momentum**

|       | Learning Outcomes |
| ----- | ----------------- |
| 19(b) | state that a photon is a quantum of electromagnetic radiation, and use $E = hf$ |

## **Photon**

<!--prettier-ignore-->
!!! definition "Definition"
    A **photon** is a quantum, or discrete packet, of electromagnetic radiation.

The energy of one photon is:

$$
E = hf
$$

Since $c = f\lambda$:

$$
E = \frac{hc}{\lambda}
$$

where:

- $E$ is the photon energy in J
- $h$ is the Planck constant, $6.63 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J s}$
- $f$ is the frequency in Hz
- $c$ is the speed of light in free space, $3.00 \times 10^8\ \text{m s}^{-1}$
- $\lambda$ is the wavelength in m

## **Photon Momentum**

A photon has no rest mass, but it has momentum.

$$
p = \frac{E}{c}
$$

Using $E = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$:

$$
p = \frac{h}{\lambda}
$$

!!! warning "Common misconception"
    Massless does not mean momentum is zero. A photon has momentum because it carries energy and travels at speed $c$.

## **Worked Example: Energy from Wavelength**

Calculate the energy of a photon of wavelength $500\ \text{nm}$.

$$
E = \frac{hc}{\lambda}
$$

$$
E = \frac{(6.63 \times 10^{-34})(3.00 \times 10^8)}{500 \times 10^{-9}}
$$

$$
E = 3.98 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{J}
$$

In electronvolts:

$$
E = \frac{3.98 \times 10^{-19}}{1.60 \times 10^{-19}} = 2.49\ \text{eV}
$$

## **Worked Example: Photon Momentum**

For the same photon:

$$
p = \frac{h}{\lambda}
$$

$$
p = \frac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{500 \times 10^{-9}}
$$

$$
p = 1.33 \times 10^{-27}\ \text{kg m s}^{-1}
$$

## **Exam Use**

- Convert nm to m before substitution.
- Use energy in J when using SI constants.
- Use eV only when comparing energy differences in eV.
