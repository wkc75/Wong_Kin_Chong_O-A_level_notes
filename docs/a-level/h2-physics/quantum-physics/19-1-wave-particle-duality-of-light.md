## **Wave Evidence**

Interference and diffraction show that electromagnetic radiation has wave behaviour.

- **Interference** occurs when waves superpose to form regions of reinforcement and cancellation.
- **Diffraction** occurs when waves spread after passing through a narrow gap or around an obstacle.
- These effects require phase and path difference, which are wave ideas.

## **Particle Evidence: Photoelectric Effect**

The photoelectric effect is the emission of electrons from a metal surface when electromagnetic radiation of sufficiently high frequency is incident on it.

<!--prettier-ignore-->
!!! definition "Definition"
    The **threshold frequency** $f_0$ is the minimum frequency of incident radiation required to emit photoelectrons from a metal surface.

Key observations:

- No photoelectrons are emitted if $f < f_0$, no matter how intense the radiation is.
- If $f > f_0$, photoelectrons are emitted almost immediately.
- Increasing intensity increases the number of emitted photoelectrons per second, provided $f > f_0$.
- Increasing frequency increases the maximum kinetic energy of the photoelectrons.

## **Why Threshold Frequency Supports Photons**

In the photon model, radiation arrives in packets of energy:

$$
E = hf
$$

An electron absorbs energy from one photon. If the photon energy is smaller than the work function $\phi$ of the metal, the electron cannot escape.

$$
hf_0 = \phi
$$

So below threshold frequency, each photon has insufficient energy. More intensity only gives more low-energy photons, not enough energy per photon.

## **Wave Model vs Photon Model**

| Observation | Wave model difficulty | Photon model explanation |
| ----------- | --------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Threshold frequency exists | Energy should depend mainly on intensity | Each photon needs enough energy, $hf \ge \phi$ |
| No delay above threshold | Energy would need time to accumulate | One photon can transfer energy to one electron |
| Higher intensity gives more electrons | reasonable | more photons arrive each second |
| Higher frequency gives higher kinetic energy | not explained by intensity alone | photon energy increases with frequency |

!!! warning "Common misconception"
    Increasing intensity cannot cause photoemission if the frequency is below threshold. It increases the number of photons, but each photon is still too low in energy.
