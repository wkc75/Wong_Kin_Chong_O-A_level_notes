## **Discrete Energy Levels**

Electrons in isolated atoms can only occupy certain allowed energy levels. They cannot have any energy value between these levels.

When an electron changes energy level, a photon is absorbed or emitted.

$$
hf = \Delta E
$$

and:

$$
\Delta E = \frac{hc}{\lambda}
$$

## **Absorption**

An electron absorbs a photon and moves to a higher energy level if the photon energy exactly matches the energy gap.

$$
hf = E_{\text{higher}} - E_{\text{lower}}
$$

## **Emission**

An electron falls to a lower energy level and emits a photon.

$$
hf = E_{\text{higher}} - E_{\text{lower}}
$$

## **Emission vs Absorption Spectra**

| Spectrum | Appearance | Process |
| -------- | ---------- | ------- |
| Emission line spectrum | bright coloured lines on a dark background | electrons emit photons when falling to lower levels |
| Absorption line spectrum | dark lines in a continuous spectrum | electrons absorb photons to move to higher levels |

## **Why Spectral Lines Are Discrete**

Only certain energy gaps exist in an atom. Since each photon energy is $hf$, only certain frequencies and wavelengths are emitted or absorbed.

Different elements have different energy level spacings, so they have characteristic spectra.

## **Worked Example**

An electron transition emits a photon of energy $3.03 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{J}$. Find the wavelength.

$$
\Delta E = \frac{hc}{\lambda}
$$

$$
\lambda = \frac{hc}{\Delta E}
$$

$$
\lambda = \frac{(6.63 \times 10^{-34})(3.00 \times 10^8)}{3.03 \times 10^{-19}}
$$

$$
\lambda = 6.56 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m}
$$

$$
\lambda = 656\ \text{nm}
$$

## **Exam Use**

- Use the magnitude of the energy difference.
- Emission is downward transition; absorption is upward transition.
- Convert eV to J if using $h$ in SI units.

!!! warning "Common misconception"
    A photon is absorbed only if its energy matches an allowed energy gap. A slightly different photon energy is not usually absorbed by an isolated atom.
